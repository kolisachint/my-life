Sachin Koli is a solution architect based in Pune, India, with eighteen years
spent building the data and cloud platforms underneath large enterprises.

He currently leads the modernisation of digital card services for a UK tier-1

several African markets.

He writes occasionally about cloud architecture and data engineering at
kolisachint.github.io.

## Long bio — the site's About page

```
I am a solution architect. For eighteen years I have worked on the layer most
people never see: the data platforms, pipelines and APIs that make a bank's app
show you the right number, or a retailer's email arrive with the right offer.

These days that work is payments. I lead digital cards modernisation for a UK
tier-1 retail bank — the features you use without thinking about them, like
adding a card to your phone wallet, freezing it when you have mislaid it, or
looking up your PIN. Moving those from on-premise systems onto Google Cloud is
less about the migration than about the patterns: how services find each other,
how APIs stay secure across two clouds, how any of it satisfies a regulator. The
piece I am proudest of is a self-serve fraud journey that let customers handle a
fraud report themselves instead of waiting on a call-centre queue. It won at the
Banking Tech Awards in 2024 and the Card & Payments Awards in 2025.

Before payments I spent twelve years in enterprise data. I migrated a retailer's
data lake off Teradata onto BigQuery. I built a customer preference API on GCP
that served millions of people and took six hundred thousand dollars a year out
of the running cost. I built enterprise warehouses for a national telecom
operator, and unified a global bank's data across several African countries into
something you could actually report on.

The through-line is that I like the whole arc — picking the platform, designing
the thing, building it, and then having the conversation that gets it funded. I
have a habit of adopting cloud services early, which is mostly how you find the
gaps before they turn into somebody's incident.

I studied Computer Science and Engineering at Walchand College of Engineering,
Sangli. I live in Pune with my wife and two children.

Find me on LinkedIn, or on GitHub.
```

## Notes on this version

**No client names, at all.** This is the version a stranger reads. The
descriptors do the work.

**No contact details beyond the public ones.** LinkedIn and GitHub links, and an
email only if you want one on the site. Not the phone number — a public page with
a mobile number on it is a spam magnet, and it is already on the documents that
matter.

**The long bio is deliberately written in the first person and plainly.** A
personal site that reads like a resume is a resume; the point of having one is to
sound like a person. If it feels too informal for you, the medium bio is the
conservative choice and works fine.

**Award entries** usually want the short bio plus a separate project description
— write that from the fraud-journey material in `resume-recruiter.md` if a
submission comes up.
