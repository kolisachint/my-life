# Where to live

_Decided 2026-08-27. Revisit only on the triggers at the bottom._

**Answer: stay in Chikhali. Do not sell D303. Solve the commute at work, not with
property.**

---

## The question you are actually asking

It presents as *Chikhali vs Hinjawadi*, but nothing about the house is wrong. The
house is close to school, owned outright enough to be paying property tax on, and
in the same municipal belt as everything else in your life. The only thing pulling
you is a **25 km, 60–90 minute commute to Sahyadri Park.**

So the real question is narrower: *is that commute worth a property transaction?*

## What a move actually costs

Round-trip friction on selling one home and buying another in Maharashtra:

| Item | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Stamp duty on purchase | 5% + 1% metro cess |
| Registration | 1%, capped ₹30,000 |
| Brokerage on sale | 1–2% |
| Moving, interiors, incidentals | 1–2% |
| **Total round trip** | **≈ 8–10% of value** |

On a Chikhali flat in the ₹60–80 lakh band — the plausible range for Anjani Gaatha,
though you know the real number — that is **₹5–8 lakh of pure friction.** Nothing
is bought with it. It is the toll for changing your mind.

At your actual savings rate of **₹20,000/month**, that toll is **25 to 33 months of
saving**. Two and a half to nearly three years of everything you manage to put
away, spent on transaction costs alone.

And Hinjawadi and Wakad trade meaningfully above Chikhali per square foot. So the
same money buys less space, or you take on fresh loan — which attacks the ₹20k
from the other side.

## What the commute actually costs

| WFO days/week | Hours/year | ≈ working days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | ~230 | 29 |
| 3 | ~345 | 43 |
| 5 | ~575 | 72 |

Real, and worth taking seriously — at three days a week you are spending about
**six working weeks a year** sitting in traffic.

But here is the thing that settles it: **you can buy that time back for free.** The
`Raise WFO exception` task already in your system, pressed properly, plus TCS
transport or a PCMC-to-Hinjawadi carpool, delivers most of the benefit of moving at
none of the ₹5–8 lakh cost. You would be paying several lakh for something an
email can get you.

That recurring task is not admin. On these numbers it is worth roughly a month of
your life per year, which makes it one of the highest-return items in the entire
system — and it is currently sitting at p4.

## The options, ranked

**1. Stay in Chikhali, attack the commute at work.** ✅
Zero transaction cost. Keeps the asset, the school, the PCMC ecosystem. Make the
WFO exception permanent rather than renewing it monthly; add company transport or a
carpool for the days you must go in.

**2. Keep D303, rent small near Hinjawadi — only if forced.**
If TCS ever mandates five days on site with no exception, a ₹12–18k/month 1BHK near
work for office nights beats a property churn: reversible, no school disruption,
no stamp duty. Note it is still painful against a ₹20k savings rate — this is a
hedge, not a plan.

**3. Move to Ravet or Punawale.** ❌
Halves the commute and keeps SNBP reachable. But it carries the *full* 8–10%
friction for a *partial* gain. Worst ratio of the three.

**4. Move to Hinjawadi or Wakad.** ❌
Solves the commute completely, and costs the most: full friction, higher ₹/sq ft,
and it uproots both children. Only rational at a much higher savings rate.

## The upstream problem

Where to live feels like the burning question. It is not. It is **downstream of a
question you have not answered.**

Your goal descriptions commit ₹1,32,000/month to investments. The P1 subtask you
wrote on 1 August 2026 says the real floor is ₹20,000. At ₹1.32 lakh a month, a
property move is a normal decision you could absorb in a year. At ₹20,000, it is
three years of savings for a commute fix that an email can deliver.

**The savings number decides the housing question.** Answer that first and this one
answers itself.

## What would change this answer

Revisit only if one of these fires:

1. **TCS mandates 5-day WFO and the exception route closes.** 72 working days a
   year changes the arithmetic. Go to option 2 before option 4.
2. **Actual monthly investing reaches ₹75,000+ and holds for two quarters.** Then a
   move is affordable rather than destructive.
3. **Srishti reaches 8th standard** — roughly two years out. After that, moving her
   before board years is its own cost, and the window effectively closes. This
   gives the decision a natural deadline: *decide within two years or it decides
   itself.*
4. **The ancestral land matter resolves.** Depending on where that land is and what
   you do with it, the whole geography may change. There is currently no next court
   date on file — that alone is worth chasing.
